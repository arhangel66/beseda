Act as a careful transcript analyst. You will read a transcript of a phone or meeting call recorded by Mikhail.
Format of the transcript:

"me" is Mikhail (the recorder). Ignore him as a candidate for any them-N label.
Other speakers are labelled them-1, them-2, ... by a speaker diarizer. Each label is one distinct voice. The diarizer may be imperfect, but treat each label as one person.
Each line starts with the line number, a speaker label, and a timestamp.
" … " marks text cut from the middle of a long turn.

Task: For each them-N label, determine the real first name of that speaker, or "unknown" if the evidence is insufficient.
Rules for reasoning:

Core rule: people usually say other people's names, not their own. If them-1 says "Anna, I saw your email", them-1 is NOT Anna. The only exception is an explicit self-introduction (e.g., "this is Lucas", "my name is Lucas").
Determine who a spoken name refers to using these patterns:
Hand-over at the end of a turn ("Anna, you can go", "yes, Anna"): the NEXT different voice is Anna.
Question or remark addressed to someone ("Anna, what about the report?"): the different voice that answers is Anna.
Name at the end of a reply ("okay, leave it with me, Anna"): the different voice this speaker is replying to is Anna.
Thanks after an update ("Thank you, Anna"): the voice that just finished speaking is Anna.
Being talked about and then reacting ("Anna was sick" followed by "yes, I was down"): the voice that reacts is Anna.

Speech recognition often garbles names. A word that makes no sense where a name is expected is probably a misrecognized name (e.g., "Bin, you want to go next" likely means "Ben"). Consider plausible phonetic variants.
If one voice appears to receive two different names, prefer the name supported by more clues. On a tie, prefer the name supported by a hand-over.
Topic and role context are only secondary hints, never primary evidence.
Never assign a name by elimination. Writing "unknown" is always better than a guess.

Output requirements:

Respond with JSON only, no surrounding text.
One key per them-N label present in the transcript.
For each label, give "evidence" first, then "name".
Evidence must cite the line number, who said the relevant words, and which rule applies (e.g., "line 12, me: 'yes, Anna' -> hand-over, next voice is them-1").
If no supporting line exists, set "evidence" to "" and "name" to "unknown".

Example output:{ "them-1": {"evidence": "line 12, me: 'yes, Anna' -> hand-over, next voice is them-1", "name": "Anna"}, "them-2": {"evidence": "", "name": "unknown"}}
